# Lesson 1: Mentoring vs Coaching vs Sponsoring

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Differentiate between mentoring, coaching, and sponsoring
- Identify when to use each approach based on context and need
- Apply the appropriate development technique to different situations
- Understand how all three approaches complement each other

---

## Introduction

Staff+ engineers are expected to develop others, but "development" isn't a one-size-fits-all approach. Understanding the distinct roles of mentoring, coaching, and sponsoring—and when to use each—is critical to multiplying your impact through others.

These three approaches serve different purposes: mentoring transfers experience, coaching unlocks potential through questions, and sponsoring opens doors through advocacy. Good technical leaders are adept in all three and know when to apply each.

## Key Concepts Summary

### Mentoring: Sharing Experience

**Definition:** Mentoring involves sharing your experience so others can leverage it for their own growth. You talk *with* them.

**Characteristics:**
- Knowledge transfer from someone with more experience
- Typically sounds like: "I think you should..." or "Here's what I did..."
- Long-term relationship (months to years)
- Broad scope: career progression, technical growth, navigating organizations
- Works best outside the management chain

**Example Scenarios:**
- A senior engineer helping a mid-level engineer navigate their first architectural decision
- Sharing how you handled a similar technical challenge in the past
- Guiding someone through career path options based on your experience

### Coaching: Unlocking Potential

**Definition:** Coaching uses inquisitive questioning to help someone achieve their own goals. You talk *to* them.

**Characteristics:**
- Helps people apply skills they already have
- Typically sounds like: "What are you trying to accomplish?" or "What's standing in your way?"
- Shorter-term and situation-driven
- Focused on specific goals or challenges
- Can be done by direct managers

**Example Scenarios:**
- Helping an engineer work through a complex debugging problem by asking guiding questions
- Supporting someone in defining their own approach to a technical challenge
- Helping a team member identify what's blocking their progress on a project

### Sponsoring: Opening Doors

**Definition:** Sponsoring involves using your influence and organizational power to advocate for someone's advancement. You talk *about* them.

**Characteristics:**
- Advocacy and opportunity creation
- Typically sounds like: "They'd be great at this" or "Let me introduce you to..."
- Requires established trust and credibility
- Opens doors that the sponsee couldn't access alone
- Increases visibility of their work to decision-makers

**Example Scenarios:**
- Recommending an engineer for a high-visibility project
- Advocating for someone's promotion in calibration meetings
- Introducing a junior engineer to senior leaders or external opportunities
- Assigning stretch projects that build someone's reputation

### When to Use Each

The choice depends on:
- **Timing:** Is this a long-term career conversation or an immediate problem?
- **Connection:** How well do you know this person? What's your relationship?
- **Context:** What does the person actually need right now?
- **State of mind:** Is the person seeking guidance, stuck on a problem, or ready for advancement?

**Decision Framework:**

| Situation | Best Approach | Why |
|-----------|---------------|-----|
| Junior engineer asks how to grow to senior | Mentoring | Requires experience transfer and career navigation |
| Engineer is stuck on a technical approach | Coaching | They likely have the skills, need help applying them |
| High-performer ready for more visibility | Sponsoring | Need organizational advocacy and opportunity access |
| Someone needs to learn influence without authority | Mentoring | Soft skills learned through experience sharing |
| Team member struggling with prioritization | Coaching | Help them develop their own prioritization framework |
| Talented engineer unknown to leadership | Sponsoring | Visibility problem, not skills problem |

### Combining All Three

The most effective technical leaders use all three approaches. For example, with a mentee you might:
- **Mentor** them on career progression and architectural thinking
- **Coach** them through specific technical challenges
- **Sponsor** them by recommending them for key projects

## Practice Exercise

After completing the materials, try this exercise (do not spend more than 10 minutes):

Think of three engineers you work with. For each, identify:
1. What stage of their career are they in?
2. What do they need most right now?
3. Which approach (mentoring, coaching, or sponsoring) would be most helpful?
4. What specific action could you take using that approach?

Write down your answers to clarify your thinking.

## Self-Check Questions

3. In what situations might you use all three approaches with the same person?
4. Why does mentoring work best outside the management chain?
5. What's the risk of only using one approach (e.g., only mentoring) when developing others?
