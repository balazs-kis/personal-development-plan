# Lesson 1: Understanding Staff+ Roles and Archetypes

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Define what staff, principal, and architect roles entail
- Identify the four staff engineer archetypes and their characteristics
- Understand where each archetype fits in different organizational contexts
- Recognize which archetype aligns with your skills and interests

---

## Introduction

Staff engineer is the first rung on the technical leadership ladder—a path that allows you to grow your impact and influence without becoming a people manager. Understanding the different archetypes of staff+ roles helps you recognize what kind of technical leader you want to become and what your organization needs.

## Key Concepts Summary

### The Four Staff Engineer Archetypes

#### 1. Tech Lead (Most Common)
```
Scope: One team or cluster of teams
Focus: Guiding technical approach and execution
Key Activities:
- Partner closely with engineering manager
- Maintain team context and relationships
- Define technical vision for the team
- Delegate complex projects to grow teammates
```

**Best for:** Engineers who enjoy being close to the code while influencing team direction. Most common first staff+ role.

#### 2. Architect
```
Scope: Critical technical domain or area
Focus: Direction, quality, and approach
Key Activities:
- Combine deep technical knowledge with user needs
- Own technical quality in a critical area
- Create architectural standards and patterns
- Guide multiple teams on technical decisions
```

**Best for:** Engineers who excel at seeing the big picture and want to focus on technical strategy rather than day-to-day execution.

#### 3. Solver
```
Scope: Complex problems across the organization
Focus: Finding solutions to difficult problems
Key Activities:
- Dive deep into arbitrarily complex problems
- Move between organizational hotspots
- Bring clarity to ambiguous situations
- Deliver results in high-stakes scenarios
```

**Best for:** Engineers who thrive on challenging problems and don't need stable team context.

#### 4. Right Hand
```
Scope: Executive-level support across large organizations
Focus: Extending executive attention and authority
Key Activities:
- Provide additional leadership bandwidth
- Represent executive in technical decisions
- Bridge leadership and engineering
- Operate with executive's authority
```

**Best for:** Engineers in very large organizations (hundreds+ engineers) who work well with executives and can handle broad organizational scope.

### Shared Responsibilities Across All Archetypes

All staff+ engineers, regardless of archetype, share these core activities:

1. **Setting Technical Direction**: Providing long-term technical guidance and strategy
2. **Mentorship and Sponsorship**: Developing other engineers and advocating for their growth
3. **Injecting Engineering Context**: Bringing technical perspective to organizational decisions
4. **Glue Work**: Facilitating collaboration, alignment, and execution across teams

### Staff vs Senior: The Core Difference

| Aspect | Senior Engineer | Staff+ Engineer |
|--------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Scope** | One team | Multiple teams or organization-wide |
| **Focus** | Execution and delivery | Strategy and direction |
| **Impact** | Direct contributions | Multiplier effect through others |
| **Authority** | Team-level technical decisions | Cross-team technical decisions |
| **Leadership** | Leading by example | Leading through influence |

## Practice Exercise

After completing the materials, try this exercise (do not spend more than 15 minutes):

**Self-Reflection: Archetype Alignment**

Consider your current work and interests:

1. Which archetype resonates most with you and why?
2. Which archetype(s) exist in your current organization?
3. What gap exists between where you are now and your target archetype?
4. What skills would you need to develop to succeed in your preferred archetype?

Write down your answers—this self-awareness will guide your development path.
