# Lesson 2: Senior vs Staff: What Actually Changes

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Articulate the key differences between senior and staff engineer roles
- Understand how your relationship with your manager evolves
- Recognize the shift from execution to strategy
- Identify the changes in how you measure success and impact

---

## Introduction

The transition from senior to staff engineer is not just "being a better senior engineer." It represents a fundamental shift in how you work, what you're responsible for, and how you create value. This lesson clarifies what actually changes so you can prepare for and succeed in the transition.

## Key Concepts Summary

### The Fundamental Shifts

#### 1. Scope of Work

**Senior Engineer:**
- Works within one team or product area
- Owns features and components
- Makes decisions affecting their team's codebase
- Solves problems given to them

**Staff Engineer:**
- Works across multiple teams or organization-wide
- Sets technical strategy and standards
- Makes decisions affecting company-wide systems
- Identifies problems worth solving independently

#### 2. Nature of Impact

**Senior Engineer:**
```
Impact Model: Direct Contribution
- Ship features
- Fix bugs
- Optimize code
- Mentor team members

Success Metric: What you delivered
```

**Staff Engineer:**
```
Impact Model: Multiplier Effect
- Enable multiple teams to succeed
- Establish patterns others follow
- Prevent problems before they occur
- Create force multipliers

Success Metric: What the organization achieved because of your work
```

#### 3. Time Horizons

| Role | Planning Horizon | Focus |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| **Senior** | Weeks to quarters | Current sprint/quarter goals |
| **Staff** | Quarters to years | Strategic technical direction |

#### 4. Technical vs Strategic Work

**Senior Engineer:**
- 80% hands-on technical work
- 20% coordination and planning
- Deep in the details
- Execution-focused

**Staff Engineer:**
- 20-30% hands-on technical work
- 70-80% strategy, communication, coordination
- Balance details with big picture
- Direction-focused

### How Your Relationship with Your Manager Changes

#### As a Senior Engineer:
- Manager provides direction and context
- Regular guidance on priorities
- Manager sets your goals
- You execute on team objectives

#### As a Staff Engineer:
- You identify strategic problems independently
- Self-directed on high-level goals
- You help set team objectives
- Manager provides air cover and organizational navigation
- You may influence your manager's strategy

### What You're Evaluated On

**Senior Engineer:**
- Technical execution quality
- Feature delivery velocity
- Code review quality
- Team collaboration
- Mentoring junior engineers

**Staff Engineer:**
- Company-level technical decisions
- Cross-team coordination and alignment
- Strategic technical vision
- Organizational improvements
- Developing other engineers at scale
- Ability to navigate ambiguity
- Business impact of technical choices

### The Skills Gap

Many engineers struggle with the transition because it requires new skills:

| Skill Area | Why It Matters at Staff+ |
|-----------|------------------------|
| **Written Communication** | You influence through docs, not just code |
| **Strategic Thinking** | You must see around corners and plan for the future |
| **Stakeholder Management** | You work with product, design, leadership regularly |
| **Influence Without Authority** | You rarely have direct authority over teams you influence |
| **Prioritization** | You have infinite possible work; choosing is critical |
| **Organizational Navigation** | You must understand how to get things done in your org |

### Common Misconceptions

❌ **"Staff engineers write more complex code"**
→ ✅ Staff engineers work on more strategically complex problems (which may or may not involve complex code)

❌ **"Staff engineers are senior engineers with more years of experience"**
→ ✅ Staff engineers perform a qualitatively different role with different responsibilities

❌ **"Staff engineers code less because they can't keep up technically"**
→ ✅ Staff engineers code less because strategic work provides more leverage

❌ **"Getting promoted to staff is the next natural step after senior"**
→ ✅ Staff is optional; many excellent engineers remain senior-level contributors

## Practice Exercise

After completing the materials, try this exercise (do not spend more than 15 minutes):

**Gap Analysis: Where Are You Now?**

Create a self-assessment across these dimensions:

1. **Scope**: Do you currently work at team-level or cross-team?
2. **Impact**: Is your impact measured by what you deliver or what you enable?
3. **Planning Horizon**: Are you thinking in sprints or quarters/years?
4. **Work Mix**: What % of your time is hands-on coding vs. strategy?
5. **Self-Direction**: Do you identify strategic problems independently?

For each dimension where you're not yet at staff level, write one concrete action you could take to start closing the gap.
