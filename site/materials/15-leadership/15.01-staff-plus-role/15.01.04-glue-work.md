# Lesson 4: Glue Work and Invisible Contributions

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Define "glue work" and understand its importance
- Recognize invisible technical contributions in your work and others'
- Navigate the tension between glue work and career progression
- Document and communicate glue work effectively

---

## Introduction

Glue work is the essential but often invisible work that holds teams and projects together. It includes onboarding, coordinating between teams, reviewing designs, filling gaps, and ensuring nothing falls through the cracks. This work is critical for success but can be career-limiting if not recognized and managed properly.

## Key Concepts Summary

### What is Glue Work?

Glue work is essential non-coding work that makes projects successful:

#### Examples of Glue Work:
- **Onboarding new team members** and helping them be productive
- **Taking notes in meetings** and distributing action items
- **Reviewing designs** before they reach implementation
- **Coordinating between teams** to prevent duplicate work
- **Updating documentation** and roadmaps
- **Talking to users** and gathering requirements
- **Noticing dropped tasks** and picking them up
- **Organizing team events** and improving culture
- **Bridging communication gaps** between stakeholders
- **Asking clarifying questions** that prevent misunderstandings

### Why Glue Work Matters

**Without glue work, projects fail:**
- Teams lack context and alignment
- Work gets duplicated or forgotten
- New engineers struggle to onboard
- Technical decisions happen in a vacuum
- Cross-team coordination breaks down

**The paradox:** Glue work is critical for success, but often invisible to management and hard to measure.

### The Career Tension

#### The Problem at Junior/Mid Levels

When junior or mid-level engineers do significant glue work:

❌ **Risks:**
- Seen as "not technical enough"
- Fewer opportunities to demonstrate coding skills
- May be pushed toward project management
- Harder to get promoted without traditional technical achievements
- Risk of being told "you might be happier in a less technical role"

✅ **Tanya Reilly's Advice for Earlier Career Stages:**
- **Reduce or stop doing glue work** to focus on technical skill development
- **Let things drop** if no one else picks them up (expose the gap)
- **Be deliberate** about what glue work you take on
- **Document** when you do glue work so it's visible

#### The Shift at Staff+ Levels

At staff+ levels, glue work becomes **expected and valued**:

✅ **Staff+ Glue Work is:**
- Strategic coordination across teams
- Architectural decision facilitation
- Technical mentorship at scale
- Cross-functional alignment
- Organizational problem-solving

**Key difference:** Junior glue work often compensates for process gaps. Staff+ glue work creates organizational leverage.

### Types of Glue Work

#### 1. Coordination Glue
```
What: Aligning people and teams
Examples:
- Running effective technical meetings
- Coordinating cross-team initiatives
- Building consensus on technical decisions
- Facilitating design reviews
```

#### 2. Context Glue
```
What: Building and sharing knowledge
Examples:
- Onboarding and mentoring
- Documentation and knowledge sharing
- Explaining technical concepts
- Preserving institutional memory
```

#### 3. Gap-Filling Glue
```
What: Catching what falls through cracks
Examples:
- Noticing and addressing dropped tasks
- Identifying risks before they materialize
- Fixing small but critical issues
- Improving tooling and process
```

#### 4. Cultural Glue
```
What: Maintaining team health
Examples:
- Fostering psychological safety
- Building team relationships
- Resolving conflicts
- Setting technical culture and standards
```

### Making Glue Work Visible

If you do glue work (at any level), make it visible:

#### Documentation Strategies:
1. **In 1:1s with your manager:**
   - "I spent Tuesday coordinating the auth service migration across 3 teams"
   - "I onboarded 2 new engineers this week, which reduced their time-to-first-PR"

2. **In promotion packets or performance reviews:**
   - "Glue Work: Facilitated cross-team architecture decisions, preventing 6+ weeks of rework"
   - "Mentorship: Onboarded 5 engineers who are now high performers"

3. **In team updates:**
   - Share impact: "The runbook I created reduced incident response time by 40%"
   - Quantify when possible: "Answered 30+ questions in #help channel, unblocking 8 projects"

4. **In design docs and ADRs:**
   - Attribute glue work: "Thanks to @person for coordinating this across teams"
   - Document the coordination process, not just the technical solution

### The Staff+ Perspective on Glue Work

At staff+ level, glue work is **strategic leadership**, not just task management:

| Junior/Mid Glue Work | Staff+ Glue Work |
|---------------------|------------------|
| Taking meeting notes | Ensuring meeting has clear outcomes |
| Onboarding new hires | Creating onboarding systems at scale |
| Filling gaps in one team | Preventing gaps across the organization |
| Responding to questions | Creating documentation that prevents questions |
| Coordinating a project | Establishing coordination patterns others follow |

**Key Insight:** Staff+ engineers do glue work that **multiplies organizational effectiveness**, not just fills immediate gaps.

### When to Say No to Glue Work

Even at staff+ levels, be strategic:

❌ **Avoid:**
- Glue work that could be systematized or automated
- Glue work that prevents others from developing leadership skills
- Glue work outside your scope that doesn't create leverage
- Glue work that's really about broken processes

✅ **Prioritize:**
- Glue work that's uniquely suited to your skills and scope
- Glue work that creates organizational force multipliers
- Glue work that unblocks high-impact projects
- Glue work that develops future leaders

## Practice Exercise

After completing the materials, try this exercise (do not spend more than 20 minutes):

**Glue Work Audit**

1. **Inventory:** List all the glue work you did in the past 2 weeks

2. **Classify:** For each item, determine:
   - Is it visible to your manager?
   - Is it recognized in your role expectations?
   - Does it create leverage (help multiple people/teams)?
   - Could it be systematized or delegated?

3. **Action Plan:**
   - Choose 2 high-impact glue work items to **document and communicate**
   - Choose 1 glue work item to **systematize** (create a process, document, or tool)
   - Choose 1 glue work item to **stop doing or delegate** (if it's not strategic)

**Example:**
- ✅ Keep: Facilitating cross-team architecture discussions (high leverage, staff-level)
- 📝 Document: Mentored 3 engineers on system design (make visible in 1:1)
- 🔄 Systematize: Created template for design docs (prevent future questions at scale)
- ❌ Stop: Taking meeting notes (delegate or rotate responsibility)
