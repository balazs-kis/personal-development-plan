## Introduction

System design interviews require active practice, not just passive learning. Reading about system design is necessary but insufficient—you must practice verbalizing your thinking, making decisions under time pressure, and adapting to feedback. Mock interviews build the muscle memory and confidence needed to perform well when stakes are high.

This lesson provides practical strategies for effective preparation and structured practice.

## Key Concepts Summary

### Why Practice Matters

**Reading alone is insufficient:**
- You can understand concepts but still struggle to apply them under pressure
- Verbalizing your thinking requires practice
- Time management is a learned skill
- Handling feedback gracefully needs repetition

**Mock interviews build:**
- **Muscle memory** for clarifying requirements and estimating scale
- **Confidence** in articulating trade-offs and handling follow-ups calmly
- **Speed** in moving through the problem systematically
- **Adaptability** when designs need adjustment
- **Pattern recognition** across different problem types

### The RESHADED Framework

A systematic 8-step approach for structuring any system design answer:

**1. Requirements (5-10 min)**
- Gather functional requirements (what the system does)
- Define non-functional requirements (performance, scale, reliability)
- Clarify scope and priorities
- Document assumptions

**2. Estimation (5 min)**
- Calculate traffic (requests per second)
- Estimate storage (total data over time)
- Determine bandwidth (network capacity)
- Plan capacity (servers, databases, caches)

**3. Storage Schema (5 min, optional)**
- Define data model
- Identify entities and relationships
- Choose database types (SQL vs NoSQL)
- Plan for data access patterns

**4. High-Level Design (10-15 min)**
- Identify main components
- Sketch architecture diagram
- Show data flow
- Explain component choices

**5. APIs (5 min)**
- Define key endpoints
- Specify request/response formats
- Map features to API calls
- Consider REST vs GraphQL vs gRPC

**6. Detailed Design (10-15 min)**
- Deep dive into critical components
- Explain implementation details
- Discuss algorithms and data structures
- Address interviewer's focus areas

**7. Evaluation (5 min)**
- Review against original requirements
- Identify bottlenecks
- Discuss trade-offs made
- Suggest improvements

**8. Distinctive Component (5 min)**
- Address unique aspects of the problem
- Go beyond standard patterns
- Show deep understanding
- Discuss edge cases

**Total time: ~45-60 minutes**

### Preparation Timeline Strategies

**3-Month Preparation (Comprehensive)**

**Month 1: Foundations**
- Week 1-2: Learn fundamentals (databases, caching, load balancing, networking)
- Week 3-4: Study key components (CDNs, message queues, blob storage)
- Practice: Read 2-3 design case studies per week

**Month 2: Pattern Recognition**
- Week 1-2: Study 10-15 common problems with solutions
- Week 3-4: Practice designing 2-3 systems independently
- Focus: Recognizing when to apply patterns

**Month 3: Active Practice**
- Week 1-2: 4-6 mock interviews with peers or services
- Week 3-4: Review weak areas, practice problem variations
- Final week: 2-3 full timed practices

**1-Week Rapid Preparation**

System design follows predictable structure—you can make meaningful progress quickly:

**Day 1-2: Core Concepts (4-6 hours)**
- Learn fundamentals: scalability, availability, CAP theorem, caching, sharding
- Study key components: databases, load balancers, message queues
- Watch 2-3 walkthrough videos

**Day 3-4: Pattern Practice (4-6 hours)**
- Study 5-7 common problems (URL shortener, Twitter, Uber)
- Note recurring patterns
- Practice back-of-envelope calculations

**Day 5-6: Mock Interviews (4-6 hours)**
- 3-4 timed practice sessions
- Focus on verbalizing thinking
- Record yourself if possible

**Day 7: Review and Refinement (2-3 hours)**
- Identify weak areas from mocks
- Review challenging concepts
- Practice communication techniques

### Effective Mock Interview Practices

**Solo Practice:**

1. **Timed sessions** (45 min)
   - Set a timer
   - Pick a problem
   - Talk out loud through entire design
   - Record yourself if possible

2. **Review and critique**
   - Did you clarify requirements first?
   - Were your explanations clear?
   - Did you explain trade-offs?
   - How was your timing?

3. **Focus areas**
   - If you struggle with requirements: practice just the first 10 minutes
   - If scaling is weak: practice capacity calculations
   - If communication is unclear: record and listen back

**Partner Practice:**

1. **Rotate roles**
   - Take turns as interviewer and candidate
   - Interviewer asks follow-up questions
   - Provide feedback to each other

2. **Use real interview questions**
   - Find questions from Glassdoor, LeetCode, HelloInterview
   - Follow the actual time constraints
   - Simulate interruptions and redirects

3. **Structured feedback**
   - What went well?
   - What needed improvement?
   - Specific suggestions for next time

**Professional Mock Interviews:**

Platforms offering feedback from experienced interviewers:
- **HelloInterview:** Guided practice with FAANG interviewers
- **Interviewing.io:** Anonymous practice with real engineers
- **Pramp:** Free peer-to-peer mock interviews
- **IGotAnOffer:** Coaching from ex-FAANG engineers

### Building a Practice Routine

**Weekly Practice Schedule (4-8 weeks out):**

- **2-3 hours learning** (concepts, patterns, case studies)
- **2-3 hours active practice** (timed designs, mock interviews)
- **1 hour review** (analyzing performance, addressing gaps)

**Final Week Focus:**

- **Daily timed practice** (1-2 problems)
- **Communication drills** (verbalizing thinking)
- **Weak area targeting** (capacity estimation, specific patterns)
- **Mock interview** (at least 2)

### Common Weak Areas and How to Address Them

**Problem: Poor time management**
- **Solution:** Use RESHADED framework with time allocations
- Practice with strict timers
- Know when to move on

**Problem: Struggling with capacity estimation**
- **Solution:** Memorize key numbers (QPS, storage, bandwidth formulas)
- Practice 10-15 estimation exercises
- Create a cheat sheet of common calculations

**Problem: Unclear communication**
- **Solution:** Record yourself and listen back
- Practice explaining designs to non-technical friends
- Use "setup and narration" framework

**Problem: Not enough depth in design**
- **Solution:** Study implementation details of real systems
- Read engineering blogs (Netflix, Uber, Airbnb)
- Deep dive into 2-3 components thoroughly

**Problem: Missing trade-off discussions**
- **Solution:** Create a checklist of trade-offs for each pattern
- Practice explaining pros/cons explicitly
- Study real-world migration case studies

### Key Numbers to Memorize

**Traffic Estimation:**
- 1 million requests/day = ~12 requests/second
- 100 million requests/day = ~1,200 requests/second

**Storage:**
- 1 tweet (280 chars) ≈ 300 bytes
- 1 photo (compressed) ≈ 200 KB - 2 MB
- 1 video minute (HD) ≈ 50-100 MB

**Latency:**
- RAM access: 100 ns
- SSD read: 100 μs
- Network within datacenter: 0.5 ms
- Cross-country network: 50-100 ms

**Availability:**
- 99.9% = 43.8 min downtime/month
- 99.99% = 4.38 min downtime/month
- 99.999% = 26 sec downtime/month

## Practice Exercise

After completing the materials, create your preparation plan (30 minutes):

**Exercise 1: Timeline Creation (15 minutes)**

Based on your interview date:
1. Create a week-by-week schedule
2. Allocate time for: learning, practice, and review
3. List specific problems to practice (aim for 10-15)
4. Schedule 3-5 mock interviews
5. Identify 2-3 weak areas to focus on

**Exercise 2: RESHADED Practice (15 minutes)**

Choose one problem (e.g., "Design a URL Shortener")

Set a 45-minute timer and work through all 8 RESHADED steps:
1. Requirements (5 min)
2. Estimation (5 min)
3. Storage (5 min)
4. High-Level Design (15 min)
5. APIs (5 min)
6. Detailed Design (5 min)
7. Evaluation (3 min)
8. Distinctive Component (2 min)

**Reflect:**
- Where did you spend more/less time?
- What was hardest?
- What needs more practice?

## Key Takeaways

- Active practice through mock interviews builds muscle memory and confidence
- Use structured frameworks like RESHADED to organize your approach systematically
- Even one week of focused practice can make meaningful difference
- Mock interviews should simulate real conditions: timing, pressure, thinking aloud
- Address specific weak areas with targeted practice (estimation, communication, depth)
- Memorize key numbers for quick capacity calculations
- Practice with partners or professional services for realistic feedback
- Build a routine: 4-8 hours weekly of learning + practice + review
- Record yourself to identify communication gaps
- Focus on thinking clearly under pressure, not memorizing solutions
- Real interview performance comes from repetition and pattern recognition
- Preparation timeline: 3 months is ideal, 1 week is feasible, consistency matters most

5. Use diagrams to communicate clearly
6. Treat it as a collaboration, not an interrogation
