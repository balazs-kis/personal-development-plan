# Lesson 1: Interview Structure and Expectations

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Understand the typical structure and format of system design interviews
- Identify what interviewers evaluate at different seniority levels
- Recognize the key signals that demonstrate strong performance
- Adapt your approach based on interview stage and feedback

---

## Introduction

System design interviews follow an open-ended, conversational format that evaluates your problem-solving approach, strategic thinking, and communication skills—not your ability to memorize solutions. The ambiguity is intentional: interviewers want to observe how you reason through complex problems without complete requirements.

Understanding the structure and expectations is crucial for performing well under pressure.

## Key Concepts Summary

### Interview Format and Structure

**Open-ended conversational format:**
- No single "correct" answer
- Intentionally ambiguous to test problem-solving
- Typically 45-60 minutes long
- Collaborative discussion rather than presentation

**Typical flow:**
1. **Requirements gathering** (5-10 min): Clarify the problem through questions
2. **High-level design** (10-15 min): Sketch main components and architecture
3. **Deep dive** (15-20 min): Explore specific areas in detail
4. **Trade-offs and scaling** (10-15 min): Discuss alternatives and bottlenecks
5. **Wrap-up** (5 min): Summary and questions

### Core Evaluation Criteria

Interviewers assess these key areas:

**1. Problem Understanding**
- Do you clarify requirements before designing?
- Do you ask about constraints, scale, and priorities?
- Can you identify what's in scope vs. out of scope?

**2. Functional and Non-Functional Requirements**
- What the system must do (features)
- Performance constraints: latency, availability, durability, throughput
- Understanding trade-offs between these dimensions

**3. Architectural Reasoning**
- Intentional component choices over random selections
- Explaining *why* you choose specific patterns
- Understanding when NOT to use certain approaches

**4. Communication Clarity**
- Ability to guide the interviewer through your thinking
- Explaining complex concepts simply
- Adapting based on feedback

**5. Trade-off Evaluation**
- Comparing design alternatives
- Justifying choices with data and reasoning
- Acknowledging limitations and downsides

**6. Scalability Intuition**
- Recognizing bottlenecks and data skew issues
- Understanding when to scale horizontally vs. vertically
- Capacity estimation and back-of-envelope calculations

### Expectations by Career Level

**Junior Engineers:**
- Fundamentals and clarity
- Basic reasoning about request flow
- Core component understanding (database, cache, load balancer)
- Simple scaling strategies

**Mid-Level Engineers:**
- Scalability and performance optimization
- Data modeling and storage choices
- Trade-off analysis
- Multiple approach comparisons

**Senior+ Engineers:**
- Failure handling and fault tolerance
- System evolution and migration strategies
- Strategic decision-making
- Organizational and operational considerations
- Cost and maintenance trade-offs

### What Strong Candidates Demonstrate

**Technical depth:**
- Real-world understanding of trade-offs
- Knowledge of failure modes
- Experience-based insights

**Structured thinking:**
- Organized approach to problems
- Clear progression from requirements to architecture
- Logical reasoning about choices

**Collaboration:**
- Treating interview as conversation
- Incorporating feedback
- Asking clarifying questions

**Maturity and judgment:**
- Knowing when to use complex patterns vs. simple solutions
- Avoiding unnecessary over-engineering
- Acknowledging uncertainty appropriately

### 2026 Trends

Modern system design interviews expect:
- **Deeper practical reasoning** than rote pattern memorization
- Understanding of **mixed architectural approaches** (microservices + monoliths, serverless + traditional servers)
- **Real-world evolution** of systems over time
- **Mature engineering judgment** balancing reliability, cost, and scalability
- Experience with **cloud-native patterns** and managed services

## Practice Exercise

After completing the materials, reflect on these scenarios (15 minutes):

**Scenario Analysis:**

1. **Junior candidate** is asked "Design Twitter." They immediately start drawing microservices without asking about scale or requirements.
   - What went wrong?
   - What should they have done instead?

2. **Senior candidate** is asked "Design a URL shortener." They spend 15 minutes clarifying requirements, then propose a simple design with a single database and application server.
   - Is this appropriate? Why or why not?
   - What level of complexity is expected?

3. **Mid-level candidate** proposes using Kafka for every component. When asked about trade-offs, they say "Kafka is scalable."
   - What's missing from this response?
   - How should they explain their choice?

---

## Key Takeaways

- System design interviews test problem-solving process, not memorized solutions
- Ambiguity is intentional—clarifying requirements is part of the test
- Expectations scale with seniority: junior focuses on basics, senior on failure handling and evolution
- Interviewers value reasoning process over perfect designs
- Strong candidates demonstrate technical depth, structured thinking, collaboration, and mature judgment
- Modern interviews expect practical reasoning and real-world trade-off analysis
- Communication and explanation matter as much as the technical design itself
