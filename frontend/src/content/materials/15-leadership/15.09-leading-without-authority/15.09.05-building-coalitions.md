## Introduction

No matter how credible you are individually, some changes require collective action. Building coalitions—networks of like-minded peers, influencers, and leaders who can make decisions happen through collective action—is one of the most powerful forms of leadership without authority.

This lesson teaches you how to build and leverage coalitions to multiply your impact as a Staff+ engineer.

## Key Concepts Summary

### What is a Coalition?

**Definition:** A loose-knit group of like-minded peers, internal influencers, and leaders who can make decisions happen by the power of collective action.

**Why coalitions matter:**
- Multiply your influence beyond your individual sphere
- Provide diverse perspectives that strengthen initiatives
- Reduce resistance through representation across teams
- Enable clear communication across organizational boundaries
- Build trust and momentum for change

**Coalition vs. working alone:**
- Individual: Limited scope, single perspective, vulnerable to organizational barriers
- Coalition: Broad reach, multiple perspectives, resilient through collective power

### Building a Guiding Coalition

**Key characteristics of effective coalitions:**
- **Diverse membership:** Include people from different roles, levels, and teams
- **Respected members:** Coalition members should have credibility and influence
- **Commitment:** Members genuinely believe in the initiative's importance
- **Complementary skills:** Mix of technical expertise, relationships, and organizational knowledge

**Steps to build a coalition:**
1. **Identify potential members:**
   - Who has stake in the outcome?
   - Who has influence in relevant areas?
   - Who brings diverse perspectives?
   - Who has complementary skills?

2. **Recruit through listening:**
   - Understand their priorities and concerns first
   - Find alignment between your initiative and their goals
   - Make the minimum viable ask initially
   - Build relationship before asking for commitment

3. **Create shared purpose:**
   - Articulate compelling vision
   - Connect to organizational priorities
   - Show how it benefits members' teams
   - Establish clear goals and success criteria

4. **Maintain momentum:**
   - Regular communication and updates
   - Celebrate wins together
   - Share credit broadly
   - Keep focus on collective goals

### The 5-Step Influence Framework

From Doug Howard's framework for managing upward and influencing cross-functionally:

**Step 1: Consider the Other Person's Perspective**
- Avoid the "engineer pitching to CEO" mistake
- Understand what they care about, their constraints, their incentives
- Frame your proposal in terms of their goals

**Step 2: Consider Your Relationship**
- Influence isn't all logic—relationships matter
- Build relationships before you need them
- Invest in understanding and connection

**Step 3: Make the Minimum Viable Ask**
- Don't ask for everything at once
- Get repeated small "yeses" to build momentum
- Start with low-risk experiments
- Make suggestions sound revocable for easier approval

**Step 4: Make it Easy to Understand**
- Avoid information overload
- Use clear, concise communication
- Focus on solutions, not just problems
- Showcase your problem-solving skills

**Step 5: Mentally Rehearse**
- Practice your pitch
- Anticipate concerns and objections
- Prepare responses
- Boost your confidence through preparation

### Navigating Internal Politics

**Political reality:** Most technical decisions aren't purely technical—they're shaped by organizational dynamics, resource allocation, and influence networks.

**Effective political strategies for engineers:**

**1. Align with Executive Priorities**
- Make your project available for existing executive initiatives
- When organizational focus shifts (e.g., toward AI, reliability), frame your work to support these mandates
- Let executives spend their political capital on your project

**2. Deliver Results on High-Profile Projects**
- Use your engineering skills to make visible projects successful
- Generate political advantage for sponsoring executives
- Earn future opportunities through demonstrated impact

**3. Build Credibility Through Doing**
- Creation is the fundamental power source for engineers
- Prove ideas through working code
- Let results speak louder than arguments

**4. Understand How Engineers Think**
To influence other engineers:
- Provide objective, evidence-based arguments
- Use data and logic, not just soft power
- Respect technical merit in decision-making
- Frame proposals in terms of engineering values (quality, maintainability, performance)

### Building Strategic Relationships

**Principle:** Build relationships before you need them.

**Strategies:**
- **Help others first:** Offer value before asking for favors
- **Be genuinely curious:** Learn about others' work and challenges
- **Create regular touchpoints:** Don't only reach out when you need something
- **Share credit broadly:** Make others look good
- **Be reliable:** Follow through on commitments consistently

**Relationship mapping:**
1. **Identify key stakeholders** for your area of influence
2. **Assess relationship strength** (strong, developing, weak, none)
3. **Prioritize:** Who are the most critical relationships?
4. **Invest deliberately:** Schedule time to build strategic relationships

### Combining Technical Excellence with Influence

**The multiplier effect:**
- Technical credibility × Coalition strength = Maximum impact
- Neither alone is sufficient at Staff+ level
- Together they create unstoppable momentum

**How to develop both:**
- **Technical excellence:** Continue deepening expertise, shipping quality work
- **Influence skills:** Actively contribute to decision-making, unlock team potential
- **Integration:** Use technical wins to build relationships, use relationships to amplify technical initiatives

## Practice Exercise

After completing the materials, create a coalition plan (25 minutes):

**Part 1: Relationship Mapping (10 minutes)**
1. List 10 people across your organization who could be relevant to your area of impact
2. Rate your current relationship with each (strong, developing, weak, none)
3. Identify the top 3 relationships that would most multiply your influence
4. For each, note: What do they care about? How could you help them?

**Part 2: Coalition Design (15 minutes)**
1. Choose a technical initiative you want to drive
2. Identify 5-7 people who should be in your guiding coalition:
   - What diverse perspectives do they bring?
   - What influence do they have?
   - Why would they care about this initiative?
3. Draft your "minimum viable ask" for each person
4. Plan your approach: How will you recruit them to the coalition?

## Key Takeaways

- Coalitions multiply your influence through collective action across organizational boundaries
- Build diverse guiding coalitions with respected, committed members from different teams and levels
- Start relationship-building before you need help—invest in strategic relationships proactively
- Use the 5-step framework: understand perspective, leverage relationships, make minimum viable asks, communicate clearly, and rehearse
- Align your technical initiatives with executive priorities to gain political support
- Combine technical excellence with influence skills for maximum impact
- Prove your ideas through working code and results, not just arguments
- Make the minimum viable ask to get repeated "yeses" and build momentum
- Navigate internal politics by understanding organizational dynamics and building coalitions
- Help others first, share credit broadly, and be reliably excellent
