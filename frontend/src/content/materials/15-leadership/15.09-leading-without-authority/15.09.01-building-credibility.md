# Lesson 1: Building Technical Credibility and Trust

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
- Understand the components of technical credibility and trust
- Apply the Trust Equation framework to build stronger relationships
- Implement strategies to establish credibility as a technical leader
- Recognize and avoid common credibility-destroying behaviors

---

## Introduction

Technical credibility is the foundation of influence without authority. As a Staff+ engineer, your ability to shape technical direction depends not on your title, but on the trust and respect you've earned. Credibility is built slowly through consistent actions but can be lost instantly through dishonesty or unreliability.

This lesson explores how to systematically build and maintain technical credibility in your organization.

## Key Concepts Summary

### The Trust Equation

Trust = (Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) / Self-Orientation

**Three trust builders:**
- **Credibility:** Your expertise, skills, and how others perceive your technical knowledge
- **Reliability:** Your predictability and dependability through consistent actions
- **Intimacy:** Creating psychological safety where people feel they can confide in you

**One trust destroyer:**
- **Self-Orientation:** Focusing on yourself rather than team goals undermines all other trust elements

### The BRAVING Framework

Rebecca Miller-Webster's framework for building trust:
- **B**oundaries: Respect others' boundaries and be clear about your own
- **R**eliability: Do what you say you'll do consistently
- **A**ccountability: Own your mistakes and make amends
- **V**ault: Keep confidences and don't share information inappropriately
- **I**ntegrity: Act from your values even when it's hard
- **N**on-judgment: Ask for help without judgment from others
- **G**enerosity: Assume the best about others' intentions

### Five Pillars of Technical Credibility

1. **Make Reliability Observable:** Publish SLOs, maintain status pages, demonstrate predictable recovery
2. **Provide Verifiable Security:** Share signed builds, supply-chain attestations, security practices
3. **Demonstrate Value with Reproducible Demos:** Show real examples with clear steps from zero to value
4. **Build Community as Social Proof:** Host office hours, share user examples, maintain public roadmaps
5. **Teach in Public:** Explain trade-offs, publish design docs, share learnings from failures

### Building Credibility Through Technical Excellence

Technical credibility is built through:
- **Consistent delivery:** Shipping quality work on time, repeatedly
- **Technical thoroughness:** Applying rigorous thinking to problems
- **Honest communication:** Presenting information accurately, admitting uncertainty
- **Personal accountability:** Taking responsibility for outcomes
- **Long-term thinking:** Making decisions that consider future maintainability

## Practice Exercise

After completing the materials, reflect on these questions (15 minutes):

1. **Self-Assessment:** Using the Trust Equation, rate yourself (1-10) on Credibility, Reliability, and Intimacy. Where is your biggest opportunity for improvement?

2. **BRAVING Audit:** Review a recent interaction where influence was important. Which BRAVING elements were present? Which were missing?

3. **Credibility Action Plan:** Choose one of the Five Pillars of Technical Credibility. What's one concrete action you could take this week to strengthen it in your work?

---

## Self-Check Questions

1. Why does the Trust Equation have Self-Orientation in the denominator rather than the numerator?
2. What's the difference between Credibility and Reliability in the trust context?
3. How can you demonstrate technical credibility before you've built a long track record?
4. What are the risks of prioritizing speed over reliability when building credibility?
5. How does "teaching in public" contribute to technical credibility?

---

## Key Takeaways

- Technical credibility is built slowly through consistent actions but can be lost instantly
- Trust is mathematical: high self-orientation destroys trust regardless of other factors
- The BRAVING framework provides a concrete checklist for trust-building behaviors
- Credibility requires both competence (knowing your stuff) and character (honest communication)
- Making your work and reasoning visible amplifies credibility across the organization
