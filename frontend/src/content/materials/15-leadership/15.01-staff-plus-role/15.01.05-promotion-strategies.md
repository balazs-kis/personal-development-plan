---

## Introduction

Reaching staff engineer requires deliberate strategy—it won't happen through excellent execution alone. About two-thirds of staff engineers get promoted at their current company, while one-third switch companies to attain the title. This lesson covers both paths and the concrete steps to increase your chances of success.

## Key Concepts Summary

### The Two Paths to Staff

#### Path 1: Promotion at Current Company (~66% of staff engineers)

**Advantages:**
- Existing relationships and credibility
- Deep knowledge of systems and organization
- Established track record
- Lower risk (keep your job if promotion fails)

**Challenges:**
- May hit organizational constraints (budget, headcount)
- Existing perceptions can be hard to change
- Longer timeline (typically 6-18+ months)
- May face internal politics

#### Path 2: Switching Companies (~33% of staff engineers)

**Advantages:**
- Fresh start, no legacy perceptions
- Can target companies with clear staff+ tracks
- Often faster (3-6 months from search to offer)
- Potentially significant compensation increase

**Challenges:**
- Must interview at staff+ level (higher bar)
- No existing relationships or context
- Riskier (giving up current stability)
- May not find the right opportunity

**Key Decision Point:** Try the promotion path first. If you hit insurmountable obstacles (no budget, no sponsor, no clear path), then consider switching.

### The Promotion Packet: Your Roadmap

A promotion packet is both a **planning document** and a **formal artifact**:

#### When to Create It
- **Start early:** 6-12 months before you expect promotion
- **Use as roadmap:** Identify gaps and guide your work
- **Update regularly:** Revise as you complete milestones
- **Formal submission:** Submit polished version when ready

#### Promotion Packet Template

```markdown
# Staff Engineer Promotion Packet: [Your Name]

## Executive Summary
2-3 sentences: Why I'm ready for staff and what I'd bring to the role.

## Staff Projects
### Project 1: [Project Name]
- **What I did:** [Your role and contributions]
- **Impact:** [Quantifiable results, teams affected, business value]
- **Complexity:** [What made this staff-level work]
- **Supporting docs:** [Links to design docs, metrics, etc.]

### Project 2: [Project Name]
[Same structure]

## High-Leverage Organizational Improvements
- **Improvement 1:** [What you changed and its impact]
- **Improvement 2:** [What you changed and its impact]

## Quantifiable Impact
- Reduced deployment time by 40%
- Saved $200K in infrastructure costs
- Decreased incident response time from 2h to 30min
- Enabled 3 teams to ship 2x faster

## Mentorship & Leadership
- **Engineer A:** Mentored on system design; now leading architecture reviews
- **Engineer B:** Coached through technical challenges; received promotion
- [Quantify mentees and their achievements]

## Glue Work & Organizational Contributions
- Facilitated cross-team technical alignment on auth strategy
- Established design review process adopted by 5 teams
- Created onboarding program reducing time-to-productivity by 50%

## Advocates & Support
- **Team X:** Survey shows 100% would recommend working with me
- **Manager Y:** "Critical to our technical strategy" (link to feedback)
- **Principal Engineer Z:** Supported my architecture proposals

## Skill Gaps & Development Plan
- **Gap 1:** Limited experience with [technology/area]
  - **Plan:** Taking course, working with Expert A on Project B
- **Gap 2:** Need more visibility at senior leadership level
  - **Plan:** Presenting at eng all-hands, joining architecture council

## Why I Want This
[1-2 paragraphs on why staff aligns with your career goals and interests]
```

#### Best Practices

1. **Quantify everything possible:** Use metrics, percentages, time saved
2. **Link to evidence:** Design docs, PRs, feedback, metrics dashboards
3. **Tell a story:** Connect projects to strategy and business value
4. **Be honest about gaps:** Show self-awareness and a plan
5. **Get feedback early:** Share draft with manager and trusted colleagues

### Staff Projects: Demonstrating Capability

A "staff project" is work that demonstrates you can operate at staff level:

#### Characteristics of Staff Projects:

✅ **Appropriate Scope:**
- Affects multiple teams or the organization
- Takes multiple quarters to complete
- Requires coordination across teams
- Has strategic business impact

✅ **Appropriate Complexity:**
- Technically complex OR
- Organizationally complex OR
- Both (ideal)

✅ **Demonstrates Leadership:**
- You drove it (even without authority)
- You aligned stakeholders
- You made and documented key decisions
- You enabled others to contribute

#### Examples of Staff Projects:

- **Technical:** Design and lead migration of monolith to microservices
- **Infrastructure:** Build multi-region deployment system used by all teams
- **Standards:** Establish and socialize API design standards company-wide
- **Tooling:** Create developer platform that reduces deployment time 60%
- **Architectural:** Redesign authentication system for 10M+ users

#### How to Find/Create Staff Projects:

1. **Look for pain points affecting multiple teams**
2. **Identify strategic technical bets the company needs to make**
3. **Propose solutions to problems leadership cares about**
4. **Volunteer for cross-team initiatives**
5. **Create a proposal and get buy-in** (don't wait to be asked)

### Building Visibility

**The harsh truth:** If you aren't visible, your work won't be valued.

#### Strategies for Visibility:

1. **Write Design Documents**
   - Share broadly, not just with your team
   - Post in engineering forums/channels
   - Present at architecture reviews

2. **Present at Eng All-Hands**
   - Volunteer to share major project results
   - Teach about technologies or patterns
   - Discuss technical strategy

3. **Contribute to Engineering Forums**
   - Participate in architecture council
   - Review designs from other teams
   - Answer questions in engineering channels

4. **External Visibility (Optional but Powerful)**
   - Blog about technical challenges and solutions
   - Speak at conferences or meetups
   - Contribute to open source
   - Build a reputation beyond your company

5. **Maintain a Brag Document**
   - Weekly log of accomplishments
   - Easier than recreating history later
   - Share highlights in 1:1s and team updates

### Finding and Cultivating a Sponsor

**Mentor vs. Sponsor:**
- **Mentor:** Gives advice and guidance
- **Sponsor:** Advocates for you in rooms you're not in

#### Why You Need a Sponsor:

Staff promotions usually require **approval at senior levels** (director, VP, or higher). A sponsor:
- Advocates for you in promotion committees
- Provides air cover for ambitious projects
- Connects you to opportunities
- Vouches for your readiness

#### Who Can Be Your Sponsor:

**Best:** Your direct manager (they're in the right meetings)
**Also good:** Skip-level manager, principal/staff engineer with influence, director in another team you've worked with

#### How to Cultivate Sponsorship:

1. **Deliver results consistently:** Sponsors need evidence you're worth advocating for
2. **Communicate your goals clearly:** "I'm working toward staff and would value your support"
3. **Make their job easy:** Provide them with talking points (your promotion packet)
4. **Align with their priorities:** Work on problems they care about
5. **Be low-maintenance:** Sponsors have limited bandwidth; don't create unnecessary overhead

### Timeline and Expectations

**Realistic Timeline:**
- **6-12 months:** Identify gaps, execute staff project(s), build visibility
- **3-6 months:** Create and refine promotion packet, secure sponsor support
- **1-3 months:** Formal promotion process (if your company has one)

**Total: 12-18+ months is typical**

**Important:** Senior to staff is not automatic. It requires:
- Demonstrable staff-level work (not just time)
- Organizational need (open headcount/budget)
- Strong advocacy (sponsor who fights for you)

### Common Failure Modes

❌ **Waiting to be promoted without signaling intent**
→ ✅ Tell your manager explicitly: "I want to reach staff and need your guidance"

❌ **Doing senior-level work faster or better**
→ ✅ Do staff-level work: broader scope, organizational impact, strategic problems

❌ **Working in isolation without visibility**
→ ✅ Build relationships, share work, present at company forums

❌ **Expecting promotion based solely on tenure**
→ ✅ Demonstrate capability through staff projects and organizational impact

❌ **Creating promotion packet only when HR asks for it**
→ ✅ Start 6-12 months early and use it as a roadmap

## Practice Exercise

After completing the materials, try this exercise (do not spend more than 30 minutes):

**Create Your Promotion Packet Outline**

Even if you're not ready for staff yet, creating an outline reveals gaps:

1. **Start with the template above** (copy it to a personal doc)

2. **Fill in what you can:**
   - What would you list under "Staff Projects"? (even if none fully qualify yet)
   - What organizational improvements have you made?
   - What mentorship have you done?
   - Who might sponsor you?

3. **Identify gaps:**
   - What sections are empty or weak?
   - What evidence is missing?
   - What skills need development?

4. **Create a 6-month action plan:**
   - What staff project could you propose or join?
   - How will you increase visibility?
   - Who will you build relationships with?

**Example Gap Analysis:**
- ❌ Gap: No staff projects (currently work only on team-level features)
- ✅ Action: Propose multi-team API standardization project to manager
- ❌ Gap: Limited visibility outside my team
- ✅ Action: Volunteer to present at next eng all-hands
- ❌ Gap: No clear sponsor
- ✅ Action: Discuss staff goals with manager in next 1:1
